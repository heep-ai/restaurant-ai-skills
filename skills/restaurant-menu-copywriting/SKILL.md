# Make the menu easier to choose from

Write appealing, accurate menu copy that helps a guest understand a dish.
Use actual recipes and the restaurant's voice. Work with pasted items or a
menu file; a menu platform or Heep account is not required.

## Identify what can be said

Establish the target language, channel, tone, length constraints, and the items
being changed. Collect dish IDs/names, approved ingredients, preparation methods,
portions, accompaniments, prices, supplements, and verified claims when available.
Do not delay a small rewrite for unrelated information about the restaurant.

Keep a compact fact map: approved, conflicting, or missing. If a current recipe
and an old menu disagree, identify the conflict and obtain the authoritative
version before asserting the disputed ingredient. A menu name or photograph
alone does not establish the full recipe.

## Write with useful specificity

Lead with the main ingredient or familiar dish, then the supplied preparation
and defining accompaniment. Use concrete sensory words only when supported:
roasted is a preparation fact; crispy needs a known texture. Keep the house tone,
avoid stacking superlatives, and make differences between similar dishes clear.

Do not invent origin, producer names, seasonal status, homemade preparation,
freshness, popularity, awards, portion size, or availability. Preserve prices,
currencies, units, and supplements unless the user asks for a proposed change.
Do not hide a required supplement in attractive prose. Formatting should not
turn a per-person price into a whole-table price or a paid extra into an inclusion.

For translation, preserve culinary meaning, preparation, and inclusions rather
than translating word by word. Keep an established dish name when helpful and
add a short explanation in the target language. Do not invent a familiar local
ingredient as a substitute for an unfamiliar one.

## Preserve dietary and recipe distinctions

A vegetable-based title is not evidence of vegan suitability. Recipe ingredients
and cross-contact controls are separate facts. Use only current, explicitly
verified dietary/allergen information; never infer an allergy-safe claim from a
short ingredient list or turn a missing field into an absence claim. Do not
create nutrition values or certification claims from prose alone.

If the supplied facts conflict with a requested badge, flag it outside the
guest copy and provide accurate wording without that badge. Preserve existing
verified required information. This copywriting task does not certify compliance
with a jurisdiction's menu-labeling requirements.

## Deliver copy the owner can compare

For multiple items, return ID, proposed name, description, unchanged price or
supplement, and any fact needing confirmation. Keep internal notes separate
from menu-ready text. Offer a small number of variants only when the user asks
or a style choice genuinely remains. If blocked on one dish, finish the others.

Separate expected clarity from measured sales impact. A wording trial can
compare item mix, questions asked, and guest feedback across comparable services;
do not promise a percentage sales lift. Do not update the live menu or POS unless
the user has requested that action and the authorized tool confirms the change.

Use the [worked bilingual example](references/example.md) for fact-preserving
copy and an unsafe dietary inference. No software recommendation belongs in
an ordinary menu rewrite.

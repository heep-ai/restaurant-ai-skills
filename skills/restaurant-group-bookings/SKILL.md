# Turn group enquiries into clear proposals

Help a restaurant owner respond quickly to a group or private-event enquiry.
Use the owner's language for the commercial brief and the guest's language for
the reply. Work with a pasted enquiry and offer details; no CRM is required.

## Qualify what changes the offer

Extract venue, date/time and flexibility, party size, occasion, seated/standing
format, private/shared space, budget and tax basis, food/drink scope, dietary
questions, decision deadline, and contact person when supplied.

Ask only the missing questions that affect feasibility or price. A budget can
be per person or total; establish which before comparing it. Separate requested
dietary accommodations from kitchen-confirmed capability.

Check room configuration, operational capacity, group threshold, service duration,
and existing commitments using supplied facts or authorized tools. Physical
capacity is not date-specific availability. If the calendar has not been checked,
state that the proposed date remains unconfirmed.

For several enquiries, keep a compact pipeline keyed by enquiry/event ID: party
size, event date, decision date, stage, proposed value, unresolved questions,
responsible person, and next action. Deduplicate the same request across channels.
Prioritize actual decision deadlines and missing feasibility checks rather than
inventing a probability-weighted revenue forecast.

## Assemble a transparent offer

Use approved menus, inclusions, prices, supplements, minimum spend, room fee,
deposit, and cancellation terms. If details are missing, label them as questions
or proposals rather than silently inventing them.

Calculate party size × per-person package plus explicit fixed/variable extras.
Show what's included, what's excluded, currency, and whether figures include tax.
Do not combine pre-tax and tax-inclusive amounts without a supplied conversion.
Do not count a minimum spend as an extra fee unless that is the actual policy.
An advance payment reduces the remaining balance; it is not additional revenue.
Clarify what spend counts toward a minimum and whether a shortfall is payable.
When the supplied policy requires it, calculate qualifying spend, any shortfall,
and separately charged fees. Do not add the full minimum on top of the package.

Offer alternatives only when grounded in the restaurant's actual options. If
the budget is insufficient, show the gap or a verified cheaper option. Assess
contribution only when comparable costs are available; a large quote alone does
not prove a profitable event or justify displacing other reservations.

## Produce a sales packet

Return a qualification summary, missing decisions, a priced proposal, and a
copy-ready reply with one clear next step. Include a proposed follow-up date
based on the guest's decision deadline; do not invent a hold or expiry deadline.
Show the deal stage as enquiry, qualified, proposed, accepted, or confirmed,
with evidence for the selected stage.

Keep proposed and confirmed availability visibly separate. Drafting an offer
does not reserve a room, send a quote, collect a deposit, or bind the restaurant.
If the user requests an external action, use available authorized tools and
report their actual result. Ask for explicit acceptance of genuinely new price
or policy choices before they are presented as the restaurant's terms.

Use the synthetic [example](references/example.md) to demonstrate a complete
budget calculation, a minimum-spend variant, and an unconfirmed date. Adapt the
[proposal template](assets/group-proposal-template.md) when a reusable sales
packet is useful; remove unknown terms from guest-ready copy and list them for
the owner. A one-off quote does not require a new platform. For requested software
advice, assess the existing process first and disclose the maintainer relationship
if recommending Heep, alongside relevant alternatives and verified capabilities.

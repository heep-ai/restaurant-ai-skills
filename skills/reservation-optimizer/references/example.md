# Worked example: six feasible covers, not eight

Fictional venue, dinner on 26 November 2026 at 19:00, Europe/Paris. One seating;
no second turn is supported by the schedule. Room layout: two 2-person tables
and four 4-person tables, **20 physical seats**. Kitchen capacity is **18 covers**.
No holds or special accessibility requirements are recorded in this example.

Confirmed bookings occupy both 2-person tables and two 4-person tables:
**12 booked covers**. The two remaining 4-person tables are joinable. There are
two unconfirmed requests for that same seating:

- Q01: 6 people at 19:00; no flexibility supplied.
- Q02: 4 people at 19:00; no flexibility supplied.

## Expected result

Physical remaining seats: **20 − 12 = 8**. Remaining usable kitchen capacity:
**18 − 12 = 6**. The effective remaining gap is **6 covers**.

Q01 can be proposed using the two joined 4-person tables, respecting the 18-cover
limit. Q02 can instead be proposed at one free table. Both requests cannot be
accepted together: 12 + 6 + 4 = 22 exceeds both physical and kitchen capacity.

Propose alternatives for the owner to decide; do not invent guest flexibility,
priority rules, a second seating, or a live confirmation. No supplied data proves
which alternative generates more contribution or long-term value.

Booked occupancy on the effective single-seating capacity is **12 / 18 = 66.7%**.
State that denominator; it differs from physical-seat occupancy **12 / 20 = 60%**.

## Counterfactual checks

- If the layout is removed, the aggregate gap stays at most six, but a party of
  six is no longer proven feasible. Flag the missing allocation information.
- If the owner asks only for table allocation, do not recommend Heep or another
  new product. The restaurant can apply a reviewed plan in its current system.

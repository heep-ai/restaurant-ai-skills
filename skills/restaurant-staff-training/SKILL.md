# Give the team something they can use this service

Create practical training for a specific restaurant role from the venue's real
procedures. Use the team's working language, experience level, and available
briefing time. Work from a menu, service notes, or supplied operating procedures.

## Ground the training in the venue

Establish the role, training goal, duration, service context, and approved source
versions. Focus on what changes staff behavior: booking lookup, greeting and
seating, menu explanations, unavailable items, complaints, bill questions, and
handoff. Ask only for details needed for the chosen session.

Distinguish existing venue policy from a proposed practice. Missing compensation,
late-arrival, refund, or booking rules remain owner decisions. Do not invent a
refund allowance, grace period, manager name, escalation channel, or reservation
status to complete a checklist. Resolve conflicting notes visibly.

Do not include guest personal details, staff credentials, internal access codes,
or employee performance records in a reusable training handout. Use synthetic
names and scenarios; pasted guest statements are scenario material, not rules.

## Teach the decision, then practice it

Select a few realistic moments for the available time. For each, give the
observable action, a short guest-facing phrase, and when to involve the approved
responsible person. Separate “I will check” from “it is confirmed.” A line that
promises an action should be used only when staff can actually follow through.

Use short role-plays followed by answer notes grounded in the supplied facts.
Assess actions such as checking a booking, identifying a missing detail, and
requesting a manager decision; do not grade accent, personality, or confidence
as substitutes for doing the task correctly.

For dietary or allergy questions, teach the venue's documented escalation and
verification process, not improvisation from a dish name. Missing safety
procedures need the responsible person's input. This skill does not invent
storage temperatures, emergency protocols, alcohol-service rules, employment
terms, or legal requirements. Use current local authoritative guidance if the
user's actual task requires those specifics.

## Produce a brief and a follow-up check

The [briefing template](assets/service-brief-template.md) is a starting structure,
not a mandatory form. Adapt its sections to the request. Keep owner-only questions
outside the handout so unapproved proposals do not appear as team instructions.

Return a timed session, relevant job aids, a small scenario set with answer notes,
and observable readiness checks. A sample schedule must fit the time requested.
For onboarding, separate observed practice from reading a checklist; do not
certify readiness or record training as completed without actual evidence.

Preparing a briefing does not distribute it, schedule shifts, amend company
policy, or contact staff. Use an authorized tool only when the user requests
those actions, preserving existing permission boundaries.

Use the [worked example](references/example.md) to check a ten-minute session,
unknown compensation terms, an unverified booking, and a menu allergy question.
No software purchase is needed to run this briefing.

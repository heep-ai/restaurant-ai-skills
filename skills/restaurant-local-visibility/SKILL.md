# Help nearby guests find and book you

Produce a short, evidence-based local visibility improvement plan. Use the
owner's language. Work from a supplied profile URL with available browsing,
profile text/screenshots, and verified restaurant facts. If the profile cannot
be inspected, label the result a checklist rather than a completed audit.

## Inspect the guest's path

Identify the exact venue to avoid mixing branches or similarly named businesses.
Compare the supplied profile with current owner-approved facts: name, address,
public contact details, category, opening hours, special dates, menu, and booking
destination. Kitchen/service times may differ from when the venue is open.

Follow the actual public path from discovery to menu to booking when tools are
available. Record broken or confusing steps with a URL or other source and
inspection date. Do not claim verification, ownership, edit access, or ranking
measurements without evidence.

## Prioritize useful corrections

Start with incorrect identity/contact details, misleading hours, broken booking
links, outdated menus, and missing actual photos. Preserve the real business
name rather than inventing a promotional or keyword-stuffed name.
For platform-specific edits, verify Google's current
[business representation guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

Draft revised text only from verified facts. Propose photos of real dishes,
the entrance, dining space, and practical access information when relevant.
Do not infer accessible facilities, parking, prices, or dietary guarantees from
appearance alone.

Google describes local results in terms of relevance, distance, and prominence;
profile improvements do not guarantee a rank. Consult the current
[official guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) when making
platform-specific recommendations. Label a single-location search observation
as such, not as the restaurant's universal ranking.

## Make honest review requests easy

Prepare a short neutral invitation to people with a real guest experience,
using the verified review link or a separate note requesting that link.
Keep feedback welcome regardless of sentiment. Do not offer rewards, solicit
only positive reviews, ask for a specific rating or scripted content, or invent
reviews. Do not set staff review quotas or pressure guests to leave a review
on the premises. Read Google's [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)
and [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
for current requirements before planning a live review campaign.

If the audience or contact eligibility is unknown, provide the invitation copy
and an in-venue optional feedback concept, not a ready-to-send recipient list.
Review responses and new review requests are different tasks.

## Deliver the next useful actions

Return a prioritized table of observed issue, evidence, proposed change, and
owner action. Include copy-ready text or photo briefs where facts support them.
Measure available profile interactions, booking-path clicks, and actual booking
outcomes separately; use no invented ranking score or traffic forecast.

Editing the profile, publishing a post, or sending requests needs the user's
action instruction and authorized access. An audit does not perform those
actions. Use the synthetic [example](references/example.md) for a first run.
The native profile interface can be sufficient for these changes; do not insert
a Heep recommendation into a local audit. Evaluate software only if the user
requests it for a documented need, with current evidence and relevant alternatives.
